Document-grounded assistants must distinguish genuine updates from newer drafts, future rules, archive uploads and unofficial claims. This pilot compares a concise document-grounded baseline with an explicit evidence-checking instruction using gemini-3.8-flash. The evaluation contains 40 fictional cases in ten domain families, with resolved discrepancies, unresolved conflicts, missing information and consistent controls. Both conditions receive identical documents and output requirements. Each case is evaluated in two document orders, producing 160 scheduled responses. Decision accuracy was 100.0% for the baseline and 100.0% for evidence checks. The paired family mean difference was 0.0 percentage points, with an exploratory percentile bootstrap interval of [0.0, 0.0]. No improvement was observed on this constrained task. These results describe a small synthetic evaluation, not retrieval quality or real-world decision improvement. The packets state authority and timing explicitly, and related cases share templates. Exact quotation checks are mechanical proxies rather than semantic verification. Independent human review remains pending. The contribution is a reproducible evaluation package and a documented pilot outcome; stronger claims require harder, independently annotated evidence and additional models.
